Blinker

01 — OVERVIEW

Simulating a hysteric oscillator that blinks red LED once per second in LTSpice, designing PCB in KiCAD, and fabricating with given components & board

02 — LEARNING GOALS
  • How should components be arranged to minimize resistance, inductance, and noise coupling?
  • How should trace widths be differentiated between power and signal?
  • How should vias pads be placed?
  • How does a hysteric oscillator work?
  • How should components be chosen according to worst-case analysis and per-unit sensitivities?
  • How do you parse through a data sheet?
  • How does reflow soldering work?

04 — REFLECTION

A big reminder this project instilled in me was how important it is to read documentation. Parsing through datasheets taught me that physical distance between components affect how circuits perform, which was something I never encountered in my Microelectronic Circuits course, where everything lived in simulation. Datasheets showed me the path between a schematic and a real board, where a bypass capacitor had to sit closely to the op amp's supply pin to provide current during fast switching and the regulator needed bulk capacitors at its input and output to maintain stability.

My favorite part of it all was assembling the board with reflow soldering. I learned that boards must follow a temperature profile--heated gradually so components don't crack, help long enough for everything to reach the same temperature, then raised above the solder's melting point so the flux can clean the surfaces and the solder can bond to each pad. I was fascinated by how a simple manufacturing step is controlled by chemistry and heat transfer as much as the circuit design, heightening my interest in process engineering where chemistry and electrical engineering collide.

I also had the opportunity to review per-unit sensitivity and worst-case analysis by applying these concepts to my own oscillator and seeing how tolerances of R, C, and feedback resistors combined to shift the blink period. This helped reinforce my previous learning.

I am definitely not 100% confident in my PCB layout skills as this was my first exposure to anything fabrication-related. To improve my design, I would decrease the overall area of the board even further and route my traces more neatly.
