RISC-V Multi-Cycle Processor

01 — OVERVIEW

Built a 32-bit multi-cycle RISC-V processor in SystemVerilog with a datapath and FSM-based control unit following Harris & Harris design; tested Icarus Verilog simulations to check all instruction types

02 — LEARNING GOALS
  • What are the multicycle trade-offs when a shorter clock period and shared hardware at the cost of more cycles per instruction are used?
  • What are the RISC-V instruction formats (R,I,S,B,U,J) and how their immediates are scambled in encoding?
  • How can a datapath and FSM that sequences control signals appropriately be designed?
  • How can modular RTL with ALUs, registers, muxes, and immediate extenders be implemented?
  • How can hardware be berified by reading waveforms to trace state, control signals, and data?

04 — REFLECTION

The difficulty of this project came from the number of moving parts our group had to keep track of: how the control unit sequences each step, how the instruction types differ, and how everything fits together modularly so every instruction works. For much of the project I was confused. Our group spent several hours discussing the control logic, and even figuring out what questions to ask was a challenge. Hearing teammates work through it gradually gave me a picture of the overall layout: a control FSM steps shared hardware through fetch, decode, execute, memory, and writeback.

The project also built my simulation skills. Checking whether an instruction worked meant reading the instr, state, reg_write, and write_data signals together, which depends on understanding the control logic. It showed me how much of hardware design is checking timing and data against what the design should be doing, and it's an area I'd like to understand more deeply.
