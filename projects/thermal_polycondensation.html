Thermal Polycondensation of Urea Into g-C3N4

01 — OVERVIEW

Synthesizing g-C3N4 from the non-metal semiconductor urea, and altering heating times to compare the final structures using an XRD and SEM for future remediation with CrVI

02 — LEARNING GOALS
  • What physical or electronic properties of g-C3N4 matter most when using this material in a photocatalytic degradation of Cr(VI)?
03 — MEDIA

This experiment was adapted from the following paper: Urea-driven g-C3N4 nanostructures, RSC Advances, 2024, 14, 26943.

Open original paper →
04 — REFLECTION

Our synthesis was based on this RSC Advances paper, in which urea is heated in a furnace until it condenses into layered g-C3N4. We kept the paper's conditions for our 4 hour control and heated our experimental sample for 6 hours. XRD confirmed that both samples were g-C3N4, and the two patterns were very similar.

Looking back, heating time was not a very informative variable to change. By 4 hours the urea had likely already condensed, so extra time did not do much. Ramp rate or final temperature would have been better choices, since they affect how the urea decomposes and how much gas is released. Our SEM comparison was also unreliable, because much less powder was mounted on the stub for the 6 hour sample, which made the images look very different from the control. That difference came from our sample preparation, not necessarily from the material. We also scanned the two samples with different XRD settings, so I had to normalize the intensities before comparing them. Next time, I would agree on the procedure beforehand, mount the same amount of powder for each sample, use identical scan settings, and record the yield of each batch. This project taught me that a comparison is only as good as the consistency between the samples being compared.
